Question Pattern and Marks Distribution:
There will be three sets of questions, each carrying seven questions.  You need to answer any five questions out of the seven questions from each set.
Q1 -> Contains 7 questions [1(a) -1(g)], each carrying 4 marks. You need to answer any 5 out of these 7. Questions will be theoretical from this part, focusing on fundamental concepts and explanations. Also, you will get 7 questions from 6 different lectures from this part.
Q2 -> Contains 7 questions [2(a) - 2(g)], each carrying 5 marks. You need to answer any 5 out of these 7. These questions will be application-based, where you are expected to follow given instructions and apply the appropriate formulas or methods to solve it.
Q3 -> Contains 7 questions [3(a) - 3(g)], each carrying 5 marks. You need to answer any 5 out of these 7. Questions in this part will be analytical. You need to put extra effort to analyze and understand the questions and answer accordingly.
Important Suggestion for the Exam:
Questions will be mostly conceptual, not straightforward. Try to have a deeper understanding of each topic rather than just memorizing.

For the theory questions, explaining the topic in your own words should be enough, but the answer needs to be correct.
You must solve each and every problem given as a practice problem in the lecture slides.
Try to see the last few semesters’ questions. However, there is no guarantee that any direct questions from the previous semester will come. But it will help you a lot.
For midpoint questions (if any), show detailed calculations. You should present them in a tabular format [(x, y), the decision variable, and related calculation]
